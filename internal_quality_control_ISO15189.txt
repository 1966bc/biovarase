INTERNAL QUALITY CONTROL (IQC) ACCORDING TO ISO 15189
======================================================

1. BASIC PRINCIPLES
-------------------
Internal quality control serves to continuously monitor the precision and 
accuracy of the analytical process, allowing the identification of systematic 
and random errors before results are reported.

Main objectives:
- Verify that the analytical system functions correctly
- Detect errors or drifts promptly
- Ensure reliability of results before release
- Document analytical performance over time


2. CONTROL MATERIALS
--------------------
Required characteristics:
- Must mimic patient samples as closely as possible
- Must cover the entire clinically relevant range
- Include levels: normal, pathological, and critical (where appropriate)

Types:
- Commercial materials (certified, ready to use)
- In-house prepared materials (require rigorous validation)

Note: For mass spectrometry, prefer materials in biological matrix similar 
to real samples.


3. FREQUENCY AND POSITIONING
-----------------------------
General guidelines:
- Insert controls in every analytical run
- Typical positioning: beginning, during, and end of session
- For LC-MS/MS: every 20-30 samples to monitor instrumental drift

Increase frequency after:
- Instrumental maintenance
- Reagent or lot changes
- Calibrations
- Prolonged activity interruptions


4. CONTROL RULES (WESTGARD)
----------------------------
System of control charts with statistical limits:

Rule        Meaning                     Action
----        -------                     ------
1-2s        Warning                     Caution, monitor
1-3s        Out of control             BLOCK result release
2-2s        Systematic shift           Investigate systematic cause
R-4s        Degraded precision         Check repeatability
4-1s        Systematic shift           Check calibration
10x         Progressive trend          Verify reagent stability

Control limits:
- Mean ± 1 SD (approximately 68% of values)
- Mean ± 2 SD (approximately 95% of values)  
- Mean ± 3 SD (approximately 99.7% of values)


5. CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
----------------------
Procedure in case of control failure:

1. BLOCK immediate release of results
2. INVESTIGATE the cause:
   - Instrumental drift
   - Reagent problems
   - Operator error
   - Environmental issues (temperature, humidity)
3. IMPLEMENT appropriate corrective actions
4. RE-ANALYZE samples if necessary
5. DOCUMENT the entire process and actions taken
6. REVIEW the procedure if the event recurs


6. SPECIFIC CONTROLS FOR MASS SPECTROMETRY
-------------------------------------------
In addition to standard controls, monitor:

Internal Standards (IS):
- Evaluate ionization efficiency
- Control extraction
- Verify signal stability
- Analyte/IS ratios must remain constant

Contamination controls:
- Carry-over between samples
- Regular blank analyses
- Selectivity verification

Chromatographic performance:
- Retention times (±2-3% of expected value)
- Peak shape (symmetry, width)
- Resolution between peaks

Matrix effects:
- Suppression/enhancement of ionization
- Use of matrix-matched curves when necessary


7. DOCUMENTATION REQUIRED BY ISO 15189
---------------------------------------
The laboratory must maintain:

□ Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for IQC execution
□ Recording of all control data
□ Updated control charts with evidence of periodic review
□ Documented procedures for handling "out of control" situations
□ Documentary evidence of corrective actions taken
□ Statistical calculations for determining control limits
□ Periodic review of limits (at least annual or after 20-30 data points)


8. BEST PRACTICES
-----------------
- Use the same lot of control material as long as possible
  (for stability of statistical limits)
- Store controls under the same conditions as samples
- Rotate the operator analyzing controls
- Do not use control data to adjust patient results
- Participate in External Quality Assessment (EQA/PT) programs
- Periodically compare your limits with those of the supplier


9. CALCULATION OF CONTROL LIMITS
---------------------------------
Initial phase (at least 20 measurements under control conditions):

1. Calculate the mean (x̄)
2. Calculate the standard deviation (SD)
3. Determine the limits:
   - Warning limit: x̄ ± 2SD
   - Control limit: x̄ ± 3SD
4. Plot the control chart
5. Update periodically with new data
